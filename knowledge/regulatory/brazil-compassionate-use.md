# Brazil compassionate use and expanded access

Verified approximately 2026-08-24 16:08 BRT (UTC-03:00).

This note describes the current ANVISA public materials for medicines. It is not
medical or legal advice and does not guide completion of patient forms.

## Current regulatory basis

ANVISA's current clinical-research regulation index continues to list RDC
38/2013 for expanded access, compassionate use and post-study supply. The legal
text displayed by ANVISA's legislation system includes later amendments,
including changes attributed to RDC 311/2019.

ANVISA's assistance-program page currently identifies three programs:

- expanded access;
- compassionate use;
- post-study medicine supply.

## Expanded access versus compassionate use

Under the definitions in RDC 38/2013:

- expanded access is a program for a group of patients using a promising
  medicine without ANVISA registration or commercial availability in Brazil,
  with a Phase III study in progress or completed for the requested indication;
- compassionate use is personal use by a patient who is not in expanded access
  or a clinical trial, for a promising unregistered medicine in clinical
  development;
- both address serious debilitating or life-threatening disease without a
  satisfactory registered therapeutic alternative under the regulation's
  criteria;
- post-study supply is a separate category for research participants after a
  study or their participation ends.

ANVISA approval for expanded access is for a group. Compassionate-use approval
is personal and non-transferable and does not allow a group in the same request.

## Who starts the ANVISA request

The current ANVISA page and Article 4 of RDC 38/2013 say the approval process
starts with a request from the sponsor or an organization representing the
sponsor, called an ORP.

The regulation defines an ORP as a Brazil-based organization contracted by the
sponsor to assume some or all sponsor responsibilities before ANVISA. The
treating physician does not replace the sponsor or ORP as the ANVISA applicant.

This makes sponsor cooperation a prerequisite to the regulatory process. ANVISA
approval is not a mechanism for a patient or physician to compel a manufacturer
to initiate a program or provide a medicine.

## Sponsor and ORP roles

The regulation assigns the sponsor responsibilities that include:

- submitting the program or acting through an ORP;
- providing the complete medicine treatment without charge;
- taking responsibility for the product and its storage before distribution;
- not commercializing the program medicine;
- monitoring distribution and remaining inventory;
- reporting serious adverse events to ANVISA;
- submitting annual and final program reports;
- maintaining free access while there is benefit according to medical judgment,
  as stated in the amended text.

An ORP may assume sponsor responsibilities under its contract, but use of an ORP
does not remove the need for sponsor participation.

## Physician role

RDC 38/2013 distinguishes a responsible physician and a treating physician. For
compassionate use, the treating physician assumes the physician duties described
by Article 19. They include:

- making a formal, medically justified product request to the sponsor for the
  patient;
- storing the medicine according to manufacturer instructions;
- reporting serious adverse events to the sponsor within the regulatory time;
- providing documentation needed for monitoring;
- assuming responsibility for medical care related to expected and unexpected
  complications or harm under the program.

For an expanded access program, the responsible physician has the corresponding
program duties. A physician's clinical justification is required, but it does
not by itself create sponsor supply or ANVISA approval.

## ANVISA role

ANVISA analyzes the sponsor or ORP submission using criteria that include:

- disease severity and stage;
- absence of a satisfactory therapeutic alternative in Brazil;
- severity of the clinical condition and comorbidities;
- risk-benefit assessment of the requested use.

For expanded access or compassionate use, ANVISA issues the applicable specific
special communication after approval. That regulatory authorization permits the
approved program under its conditions. It is separate from the sponsor's earlier
decision to submit and supply the product.

## Current forms

ANVISA's forms page was modified March 3, 2026. It currently links:

- assistance-program report template;
- Expanded Access Form, Annex II;
- compassionate-use petition, Annex III;
- post-study supply petition, Annex IV;
- compassionate-use patient presentation, Annex V;
- sponsor responsibility and commitment declaration, Annex VI;
- physician responsibility and commitment declaration, Annex VII;
- estimated import quantity form, Annex VIII;
- patient information and adhesion term, Annex IX.

The compassionate-use dossier described in the regulation includes the sponsor
petition, physician-signed patient presentation with clinical justification,
sponsor and physician responsibility forms, physician qualifications, import
quantity estimate, supporting safety and efficacy information and the signed
patient information/adhesion term.

This list documents form existence and purpose. Form selection and completion
belong to the sponsor, ORP, physician and regulatory process.

## Regulatory authorization versus product supply

The following are not equivalent:

```text
a legal pathway exists
  != a sponsor agrees to submit
  != ANVISA approves the submission
  != import steps are complete
  != medicine is available for administration
```

Once a sponsor obtains approval for a program, the regulation imposes supply and
monitoring duties. Before that, the existence of RDC 38/2013 does not establish
that any company has agreed to create a program for a specific medicine.

## What the sources do not establish

The reviewed materials do not establish:

- an active Brazil program for ION717, PrP-siRNA or any other specific prion
  investigational medicine;
- willingness by a foreign sponsor to submit a Brazil program;
- approval, import or supply for a particular person;
- individual eligibility or a favorable risk-benefit assessment;
- safety or efficacy of an investigational medicine.
