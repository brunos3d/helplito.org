## Study design and intervention

The structured design is non-randomized, sequential, open-label and single
ascending dose, with treatment as the primary purpose.

The registry describes two arms:

- **Arm 1, observational:** no investigational drug; baseline, week 4 and week 8
  study activities. The record says Arm 1 opens whenever Arm 2 is not open and
  that Arm 2 is prioritized.
- **Arm 2, experimental:** one intrathecal dose of PrP-siRNA, with sequential
  dose levels of 50, 100 and 200 mg and follow-up through week 24.

The registry describes PrP-siRNA as a divalent siRNA designed to target PRNP
mRNA. These are design and investigated-mechanism facts, not evidence of safety
or efficacy.

## F-PRISM-001 — Registry enrollment versus treated cohort

ClinicalTrials.gov estimates 30 total participants. Broad's April 22, 2026
announcement explicitly says the initial phase will enroll 15 symptomatic
patients who each receive one dose and an observational arm of 15 additional
participants who receive no treatment.

The sources therefore support this reconciliation:

```text
15 treated participants
  + 15 observational participants
  = 30 total estimated registry enrollment
```

The Broad source resolves the earlier hypothesis directly; the split is not an
inference from arm labels alone.

## Eligibility published by the registry

The current public record lists these key inclusion criteria:

1. clinically manifested symptoms of prion disease in the investigator's
   opinion;
2. probable prion disease according to CDC criteria;
3. positive CSF RT-QuIC or PRNP genetic test;
4. no more than moderate functional impairment, quantified as MRC-PDRS
   `>= 15`; and
5. an available study partner to assist with study procedures.

It also lists key exclusions including pregnancy, contraindication to lumbar
puncture and recent participation in another prion-disease clinical trial. The
registry says additional criteria apply and are evaluated at screening.

No comparison to any individual's condition or score is made here.
