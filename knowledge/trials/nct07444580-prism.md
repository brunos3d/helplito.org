# PRiSM / NCT07444580

Verified approximately 2026-08-24 15:54 BRT (UTC-03:00). This record
describes the registry state at that time. It does not assess any individual's

The registry describes the study as first-in-human. It includes an observational
arm without investigational drug and a single-ascending-dose arm.

## Sponsor

ClinicalTrials.gov identifies the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard as the lead
sponsor and responsible party. It identifies the National Institute of
Neurological Disorders and Stroke, NINDS, as a collaborator.

The Broad Institute's April 22, 2026 announcement says NINDS funds the clinical
trial through NeuroNEXT under grant `3OT2NS138339-01S2`. The registry links the
related NIH identifier `OT2NS138339`.

## Study design

Arm 1 is observational. Participants do not receive investigational drug and are
followed for eight weeks after baseline. The registry says Arm 1 is open when
Arm 2 is not open and that Arm 2 is prioritized.

Arm 2 evaluates a single intrathecal dose of PrP-siRNA. The record lists
sequential dose levels of 50, 100 and 200 mg and follow-up through week 24.

The registry describes PrP-siRNA as a divalent siRNA designed to target PRNP
mRNA. These facts describe study design and mechanism under investigation. They
do not establish efficacy.

## Eligibility published by the registry

The public record lists these key inclusion criteria:

1. clinically manifested symptoms of prion disease in the investigator's
   opinion;
2. probable prion disease according to CDC criteria;
3. positive CSF RT-QuIC or PRNP genetic test;
4. no more than moderate functional impairment, quantified as an MRC-PDRS score
   `>=15`;
5. an available study partner who can assist with study procedures.

The full name of MRC-PDRS is Medical Research Council Prion Disease Rating
Scale. UCL and the validation publication describe it as a 20-point functional
scale. The trial registry says additional inclusion and exclusion criteria apply
and will be evaluated at screening.

## Conflicts and changes over time

ClinicalTrials.gov estimates total enrollment of 30. The Broad announcement says
the Phase 1 trial will enroll 15 patients. Because the registry includes both an
observational arm and a drug arm, the numbers may use different scopes, but the
reviewed sources do not state that reconciliation explicitly.

The record should be rechecked whenever recruitment is discussed. A listed
status is temporally bounded and can change independently at each site.
