# Q-PRISM-002 — Is the reported 16/20 score the PRiSM MRC-PDRS?

Is the publicly reported `16/20` score the same clinical instrument as the
MRC-PDRS used in PRiSM eligibility?

## Current evidence

The registry confirms a key functional inclusion criterion of `MRC-PDRS >= 15`
and says additional criteria are evaluated at screening.

UCL and the validation publication identify MRC-PDRS as the 20-point Medical
Research Council Prion Disease Rating Scale. Higher scores represent better
function.

A separate secondary report attributes to the family a statement that one
study requires at least 15 points on a 0-to-20 scale and that Lito had 16. The
report does not identify the instrument as MRC-PDRS, name an assessor, give an
assessment date or establish that the score was produced under PRiSM screening
rules.

## Falsification attempt

The registry's eligibility and design modules were checked for a public
individual-score comparison, assessment visit, assessor, source respondent or
handling of a changing score. None was located. Searches located the attributed
secondary report but no primary clinical document identifying the reported
score's instrument.

## What would resolve this question

A primary, authorized statement identifying the instrument and assessment date,
or a formal PRiSM screening record disclosed by the patient or authorized team.
No private medical record is needed in this repository.

## Interpretation limits

A 20-point range and a threshold of 15 are not enough to establish instrument
identity. Even a confirmed MRC-PDRS value would not establish full eligibility,
enrollment, acceptance or treatment allocation. The `16/20` detail is not a
candidate for the public site on the present evidence.
